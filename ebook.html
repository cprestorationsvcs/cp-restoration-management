7 Steps to Fix Your Credit
in 90 Days

The exact process we use to help our clients remove negative items, raise their scores, and qualify for loans, apartments, and more.

Congratulations on taking the first step. Most people spend years frustrated by bad credit — denied for apartments, paying higher interest rates, and feeling stuck. The truth is, fixing your credit is not as complicated as the credit bureaus want you to think.

This guide walks you through the exact 7-step process our team uses to help clients remove negative items and raise their scores. We have done this for over 4,900 clients since 2014. Now you have the roadmap.

Important: This guide covers the legal dispute process under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). You have rights. Use them.

1

Pull All Three Credit Reports

You cannot fix what you cannot see. Start by getting your free credit reports from all three bureaus — Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion.

  • Go to AnnualCreditReport.com — the only official free source
  • Download all three reports (they are often different)
  • Print or save them — you will reference these throughout the process
💡 Tip: Do NOT use Credit Karma or similar apps as your only source. They sometimes miss items or show outdated information. Always get the official bureau reports.
2

Identify Every Negative Item

Go through each report line by line and highlight every negative item. This includes:

  • Late payments (30, 60, 90+ days late)
  • Collections and charge-offs
  • Judgments and tax liens
  • Repossessions and bankruptcies
  • Hard inquiries from the past 2 years
  • Accounts that do not belong to you
💡 Tip: Studies show that 79% of credit reports contain errors. Look for accounts you do not recognize, wrong balances, or duplicate entries. These are often removable immediately.
3

Understand What Can Be Disputed

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), you have the right to dispute any item that is inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable. The bureau must investigate and remove the item if they cannot verify it.

  • Inaccurate information — wrong dates, amounts, or account status
  • Unverifiable items — if the creditor cannot prove the debt is yours
  • Outdated items — most negative items must be removed after 7 years
  • Identity errors — accounts belonging to someone with a similar name
4

Write and Send Your Dispute Letters

Send a separate dispute letter to each bureau for each item you are challenging. Your letter must include:

  • Your full name, address, date of birth, and last 4 of SSN
  • The specific item you are disputing (account name, account number)
  • The reason for the dispute (inaccurate, unverifiable, etc.)
  • A request for removal or correction

Send letters via certified mail with return receipt so you have proof of delivery.

💡 Tip: Be specific. Vague disputes are easily dismissed. State exactly what is wrong and what you want corrected. Include copies (not originals) of any supporting documents.
5

Follow Up Within 30 Days

Bureaus are required by law to investigate your dispute within 30 days (sometimes 45 days if you provide additional information). After 30 days:

  • Check your mail for a response from each bureau
  • If the item was removed — great, move to the next one
  • If the dispute was denied — review their reason and escalate
  • If no response — send a follow-up letter referencing your certified mail receipt
6

Escalate Difficult Items

Some items are harder to remove. If the bureau upholds the item, you have additional options:

  • File a complaint with the CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau) at consumerfinance.gov
  • Send a debt validation letter directly to the creditor — they must prove the debt is valid
  • File an FTC identity theft report at IdentityTheft.gov if the account is not yours
  • Request a method of verification — ask the bureau exactly how they verified the item
💡 Tip: Creditors often cannot verify old debts, especially after a sale to a collection agency. If they cannot verify, it must be removed.
7

Rebuild While You Dispute

While disputes are being processed, start rebuilding positive credit history. Every positive account you add works in your favor.

  • Get a secured credit card — use it for small purchases and pay in full every month
  • Become an authorized user on a trusted family member's account
  • Pay all current bills on time — payment history is 35% of your score
  • Keep credit utilization below 30% of your available limit
  • Do not open multiple new accounts at once — each hard inquiry can lower your score temporarily
💡 Tip: Most of our clients see their first score increases within 30-60 days of starting this process. Stay consistent — credit repair is a marathon, not a sprint.
